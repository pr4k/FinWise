# Financial number design policy

- Lead with the decision value. Use large, tabular figures for account balance, card amount owed, payment due, and the four monthly summary measures. Put basis, date, and coverage in smaller nearby text.
- Keep assets and liabilities separate. Bank and cash balances use the asset treatment; credit cards show **amount owed** and an optional statement **payment due** with its due date. A card payment is a transfer, not new spending.
- Use semantic colors consistently: green for positive asset or income, warm amber for debt or spending, red for negative balances or discrepancies, and neutral gray when a balance is unknown. Always include a text label; color alone must never convey meaning.
- Align money by decimal place where possible and use tabular numerals. Keep the currency visible, and avoid combining different currencies into one total.
- Show comparisons visually before the detailed table. The monthly income and spending chart uses separate colors and exact values in its labels and tooltips. Category flows and tables remain available for detail.
- Balance anchors appear as dated events with an observed amount. They have no transaction movement and must not be counted as income or spending. When date-only transactions share one timestamp, show their count and combined movement next to the after-group balance so no single row appears to cause the whole change.
